# Muscat 2040: Executive Summary

_Author: Walid LeKheouayene_

_Prepared for Muscat Governorate decision-makers. Based on NCSI Muscat data series and the Muscat 2040 interactive model._

---

Muscat's population crossed 1.5 million in 2024. Three planning scenarios put it somewhere between **1.68 million and 2.38 million by 2040** — a range that reflects genuine uncertainty about labour migration, not a margin of error in the maths.

The model runs separate growth projections for Omani residents (natural increase, relatively stable) and non-Omani residents (labour migration, historically volatile). Non-Omani numbers fell by roughly 140,000 between 2019 and 2020 before recovering by 2023. That volatility explains why the high and low scenarios diverge so sharply by 2040.

| Scenario | 2040 Population | Change from 2024 |
| -------- | --------------: | ---------------: |
| Low      |       1,682,309 |  +183,788 (+12%) |
| Base     |       1,981,300 |  +482,779 (+32%) |
| High     |       2,376,552 |  +878,031 (+59%) |

---


## Healthcare

Muscat had **1,608 hospital beds in 2023** (NCSI). At the 2023 population of 1,455,680, that works out to about 1.10 beds per 1,000 residents. By 2024 the population had already grown to 1,498,521. This means the system was already short on beds before the forecast period even started.

**Capacity breach year: 2024 across all scenarios.**

| Scenario | 2040 Population | Beds Required | Current Capacity |    Gap |
| -------- | --------------: | ------------: | ---------------: | -----: |
| Low      |       1,682,309 |         1,859 |            1,608 |   +251 |
| Base     |       1,981,300 |         2,189 |            1,608 |   +581 |
| High     |       2,376,552 |         2,626 |            1,608 | +1,018 |

The 1.10 beds per 1,000 standard is conservative. Oman's national planning target is closer to 2.0 per 1,000. At that standard, the base scenario gap reaches roughly 2,355 beds by 2040, instead of 581. The interactive model lets planners test this directly.

## Education

Government schools enrolled **154,039 students across 4,666 classes** with **10,437 teachers** in 2024. Government-school demand tracks the Omani population, which is the group that predominantly uses public schools.

**Capacity breach year: 2025 across all scenarios.**

| Scenario | 2040 Classes Needed | Capacity |    Gap | 2040 Teachers Needed |    Gap |
| -------- | ------------------: | -------: | -----: | -------------------: | -----: |
| Low      |               5,665 |    4,666 |   +999 |               12,672 | +2,235 |
| Base     |               6,426 |    4,666 | +1,760 |               14,373 | +3,936 |
| High     |               7,281 |    4,666 | +2,615 |               16,287 | +5,850 |

Schools can manage class sizes in the short term. Raising the average class from 33 to 35 students delays the breach by a couple of years. Teacher capacity is harder to stretch. Training new teachers takes 4 to 6 years; the base scenario requires roughly 3,936 additional teachers by 2040.

---

## Recommended actions

1. Begin healthcare infrastructure planning in this budget cycle.
   Hospital construction takes 5 to 10 years from approval to operation. If planning starts in 2026, new hospitals won't open until around 2031, when the base scenario already shows a 315-bed deficit. The gap exists today; the planning response should start today.

2. Manage education staffing and facilities as one problem, not two.
   Education planning often focuses on buildings. Our data shows staffing pressure grows faster than physical capacity. Building new classrooms without hiring teachers leaves the buildings half-empty. You need to hire teachers at the same time you approve construction.

3. Refresh this model annually before the budget cycle.
   Migration trends in Muscat shifted sharply between 2019 and 2023. NCSI publishes updated data each year. The model is built to accept updated `.xlsx` files without code changes. Refreshing inputs before each planning round keeps the scenarios grounded in actual data rather than three-year-old assumptions.
